Method The blur is not a post-process. A gather blur can only spread the pixels it was handed, and an out-of-focus mote arrives as one pixel — so it leaves as a smudge. What a lens actually does to a point of light is project the aperture itself, at a size set by how far out of focus the point is. So every mote carries its own circle of confusion, solved from the thin-lens equation against a focus distance that will not hold still, and is drawn as a disc that wide, dimmed as its energy spreads. The motion underneath is boids — separation, alignment, cohesion — over a spatial hash, stirred by a slow curl field so it drifts rather than shoals. The cursor is a hand in the beam: it swirls and entrains rather than pushing, arrives on a cascaded envelope so it can never snap on, and leaves on a time constant ten times longer than it came.
