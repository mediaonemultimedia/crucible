Spine 01

Harmonics 11 / 7 / 13 of one lap — coprime with p and q, so the wobble never resonates with the knot, and integer, so the spine stays closed.

The degenerate case — p = 1, q = 0. Same expression as the knot, with the tube winding switched off.

Open spine. There is no seam and no holonomy, so the closure step is skipped entirely — not applied and found to be small.

Integer frequencies keep it closed. The phase offset is what stops it collapsing into a plane curve.

Seven control points on a closed centripetal Catmull-Rom, placed by a deterministic hash — a spine that reshuffled itself would make the residual unreproducible.

Cross-section 02

A different exponent per axis is what stops the sweep reading as cable: the wide faces hold one broad highlight, the corners snap.

One radius on both axes. The plain round cable — the reading the superellipse exists to avoid.

Equal-arc-length sampling matters most here — the corners are the profile.

A superellipse squashed hard on one axis. The twist becomes the whole subject, because a flat section has an orientation a round one does not.

Mesh 03

Whole turns close on any profile. The fraction in the reported twist comes entirely from unwinding the residual — never from an authored fraction.

Method A cross-section carried along a spine on a parallel-transport frame — each step the minimum rotation onto the new tangent, and nothing else, so it cannot flip where a Frenet frame would invert. The cost is that it does not close: come back around a loop and the frame has rotated by the path's holonomy. That residual is measured and unwound linearly across the sweep. On an open spine there is none, and the step is skipped.
