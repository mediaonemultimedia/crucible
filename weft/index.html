Weave 03

Take bend to zero and the sheet stops holding a crease — it ripples, but it will not fold. Take shear to zero and the weave racks over into a parallelogram. Both are what separate cloth from a rubber membrane.

Forces 04

Wind enters as n · (w − v) pushed back along the particle normal — a panel edge-on to the flow catches nothing. That asymmetry is why a flag ripples rather than simply leaning downwind.

Wind 05

The turbulent part is the curl of three noise potentials, which makes it divergence-free — no sources, no sinks, nothing the cloth can be sucked into. The mean flow's bearing walks through its own slower noise, so gusts arrive off-axis.

Surface 06

The cloth is double-sided, so where it folds over on itself you are looking at the back of the weave, lit from the other side.

Session 07

Self-collision is out of scope. Where a fold closes hard the sheet passes through itself, and that is the accepted cost of keeping the whole solve inside one frame.

Method A grid of particles integrated by Verlet and relaxed as position-based dynamics against three constraint families — structural along the weave, shear across both diagonals of every cell, and bend across a skipped neighbour. Bend is the one that matters: it gives the sheet a memory of flatness, which is what turns noise into folds that hold and then unwind. The wind is the curl of three noise potentials riding a mean flow whose bearing walks through a slower noise, so it gusts off-axis instead of pulsing.
