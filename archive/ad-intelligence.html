Logo & Visual Hierarchy

Where you put your logo changes everything

Logo placement affects brand recall differently depending on your campaign goal. Size, position, and timing in video all have measurable impact.

LOGO

Best for brand awareness. Western eye lands here first. Highest unaided recall.

Common in display ads. Performs well in right-rail placements but weaker for mobile.
LOGO
Ad Content Area
Center
Avoid for most service ads. Competes with the message. Blocks the visual story.
Logo Size Rule
Logo should occupy 10–15% of total ad area for display. Too large = amateur. Too small = forgettable.
NEVER:
• Place logo within 10% of any edge (safe zone)
• Use a logo with inadequate contrast against background
• Scale logo above 20% — it dominates and reduces message space
Video Logo Timing
Show logo within the first 3 seconds and again at the end. Mid-ad logo drops have poor recall. For 6-second bumpers: logo visible from frame 1.
Clear Space Rule
Maintain clear space equal to the height of the logo mark on all four sides. Never crowd the logo with text or imagery.

Frequency & Fatigue

Too much is as bad as too little

Ad frequency directly affects performance. Under-exposed audiences don't convert. Over-exposed audiences develop negative brand sentiment — and you're paying for it.

Performance vs. Frequency
Relative engagement, recall, and sentiment over impression count
1–2×
Awareness Zone
Brand registers but weak recall. Insufficient to drive action for considered services.
3–7×
⭐ Sweet Spot
Peak consideration and conversion zone. Recall is strong, sentiment is positive, CTR is at its highest.
8–10×
Saturation Zone
Diminishing returns. CTR drops, CPM may rise. Audience is tuning out. Time for creative refresh.
11×+
Ad Fatigue
Negative brand sentiment risk. Hide rates increase. You are now paying to damage your brand.
Creative Refresh Schedule
Display ads: Refresh every 6–8 weeks
Social/feed: Refresh every 2–4 weeks
Video: Refresh every 8–12 weeks
Signal to refresh: CTR drops 30%+ from launch baseline, or CPM climbs 20%+ unexpectedly.
Running Multiple Creatives
Run 3–5 variants simultaneously. This extends effective reach before fatigue, surfaces your best performer, and gives the algorithm room to optimize. A/B test one variable at a time: headline, visual, or CTA.
Frequency Caps to Set
Facebook/Instagram: cap at 5–7 per week per user. Google Display: cap at 3–5 per day. Uncapped campaigns on small audiences will destroy performance within 10–14 days.
Campaign Duration Reality
Don't judge a campaign before 4 weeks. The first 7–10 days are the algorithm learning phase. Results in week 1 are often 40% worse than week 3 steady state.

Rule of thumb: Design for silent first on Meta feed. Design for sound-on first on TikTok and YouTube. Then add the other layer as a bonus.
🎙️ Voiceover — when to use it
Use VO when:
→ Explaining a complex service (utilities, insurance, energy plans)
→ Platform is sound-on (YouTube, TikTok)
→ The emotional arc needs voice to guide it
→ Building credibility through personality or expertise
Skip VO when:
→ Simple offer ad — text supers are sufficient
→ Feed placement where 85% watch silent
→ Music alone creates a stronger brand atmosphere
🎵 Sound Bed Impact
Music increases emotional engagement by up to 30%. Brand-congruent music (music that actually fits your personality) boosts brand recognition by 46%. Irrelevant or generic stock music has near-zero effect — or negative.
For service brands: warm acoustic or soft ambient for trust → elevated tempo for urgency. Avoid: recognizable corporate stock, trending pop that will date in 6 months.
📐 Motion Principles
Animated display ads get 267% more interaction than static — but motion that adds no meaning is just distraction.
Kinetic type — text revealing in sync with VO
Reveal animation — offer or CTA emerging
Subtle parallax — depth illusion, premium feel
Loop animation — subtle infinite repeat for display
One motion focal point. Everything else holds still.
⚙️ Motion Rules & Limits
Google Display: max 3 animation loops, 15s max duration
First frame: must work as static (Gmail, low-bandwidth)
Motion direction: guide the eye toward the CTA
Speed: too fast loses the message; too slow loses attention
Reduced motion: always provide a static fallback
🔊 Sonic Logo / Audio Mark
A consistent audio signature — even just 1–2 seconds — increases brand recognition by 46% over non-branded audio. It becomes particularly powerful in 6-second bumpers where you have no time for explanation.
Think of it like a visual logo but for ears. Intel, Netflix, and McDonald's all have one. A co-op or service brand can create one for very little cost.
Pair your sonic mark with your visual logo for compound recall.
6-Second Bumper Ad Blueprint
Non-skippable YouTube format — 90% brand recall vs. 68% for 30-second ads. Every second counts.

Hook
Motion, face, or bold brand colour. Pattern interrupt.
1–4s
Single message
ONE idea only — problem OR solution, not both. Say it visually and verbally simultaneously.
4–5.5s
Brand + CTA

5.5–6s

Brand + sonic logo. Hold 0.5s on your mark.
Bumper sequencing
Priming sequence: Run a 15–30s first, bumper follows as the reminder. Most effective overall.
Teaser sequence: Bumper runs first as a curiosity hook, full ad explains. Drives higher completion on the follow-up.
Isolation: Multiple bumpers, each with one idea, run concurrently. Works for brand campaigns with a clear system.
Bumper do's
✓ Logo visible from frame 1
✓ Sound-on assumed — use music and VO
✓ One message, one visual, one CTA
✓ End on brand with your sonic mark
✓ Always pair with a longer-form ad
Bumper don'ts
✗ More than one idea
✗ Reading a long headline aloud
✗ Slow logo reveal (you have 6 seconds)
✗ Running bumpers cold (no prior exposure)
✗ Complex CTA ("Call us, visit our website, and…")

CTA copy rules
First-person outperforms second-person by 90% in some tests — "Start my free assessment" vs. "Start your free assessment"
Specificity: "Get my free 15-min assessment" beats "Contact us" every time
Numbers: "Save 20% today" outperforms "Save money today" by 2–3×
Urgency words ("today", "now", "this week") increase CTR 20–40% — use sparingly or they lose power

Copy Strategy

Direct or whimsical — when to use each

There's no universally correct tone. The right approach depends on campaign stage, audience temperature, and what you want them to feel. Here's the framework.

→ Be Direct When...
You have a specific offer, price, or deadline to communicate
Audience is already problem-aware (retargeting, search)
Competitive market where clarity beats creativity
CTA is simple: sign up, call, switch, save
Running conversion or lead-gen campaigns
Audience is skeptical or comparison-shopping
✦ Be Whimsical / Ethereal When...
Building brand awareness with cold audiences
Differentiating emotionally in a commoditized category
The benefit is felt, not calculated (comfort, peace of mind, community)
Running social/organic-adjacent content
Audiences are younger and respond to personality over features
You want to earn attention, not demand it
Headline Examples — Service Brand Context
Direct Headlines
Price / Savings
"Cut your energy bill by 20%. Guaranteed."
Works in competitive markets. Specific numbers outperform vague claims by 2–3×.
Problem → Solution
"Tired of surprise bills? Lock in your rate today."
High resonance for retargeting audiences. Agitates pain, then resolves.
Urgency / Limited
"This rate expires April 30. Don't miss it."
Use sparingly. Works for seasonal promotions. Over-use erodes trust.
Social Proof
"Trusted by 47,000 Alberta homes since 1998."
Authority + local specificity = high trust signals for new audiences.
Whimsical / Brand Headlines
Emotion / Feeling
"Power that shows up before the sun does."
Evocative. Works for awareness. Positions the brand as dependable without saying "reliable."
Community / Identity
"Built by neighbours, for neighbours."
Perfect for co-op brand differentiation. Emotional moat that a corporate competitor can't replicate.
Curiosity / Intrigue
"What if your energy company was actually on your side?"
Question format creates pattern interruption. High thumb-stop rate in feed environments.
Seasonal / Ambient
"Prairie winters hit different. So does our service."
Hyperlocal and confident. Resonates strongly in Alberta context. Builds in-group feeling.
