04 · Impersonation Risk

A scam network is already wearing four utility names.

Lead-gen operations running as "Here Comes The Solar," "Sign and Shine," and "Clean Power News" are actively spoofing real Alberta utilities in their ad copy — using the brand's own name to sell an unrelated $0-down solar pitch. This is a province-wide pattern, not something confirmed inside EQUS's own service area yet. But the tactic is brand-agnostic: it goes wherever a trusted utility name generates clicks.

05 · Recommended Moves

Five ways to close the gap.

Ranked by how directly each addresses a gap shown above.

1
Put a number in the ad.
EQUS is the only brand in the positioning map with zero offer intensity. Easymax and Encor both lead with a figure ($75, "50% off"). A concrete member benefit — capital credit, guaranteed local rate, waived fee — closes the quadrant gap without abandoning the trust position.
2
Get ahead of the impersonation network.
Four brands are already spoofed by the same solar-scam operation. A short "how to spot a fake EQUS offer" post is cheap, on-brand, and turns a live threat into a trust-building moment before it's needed.
3
Double the ad cadence.
EQUS runs 12 active variants against Encor's 70 and Easymax's 44. Volume itself is a signal in the Meta auction — matching even half of Fortis's 17 would close real ground at low cost.
4
Defend the farm/REA audience directly.
Legacy Energy's single ad names REAs and the Rate of Last Resort by name and links to a dedicated /farm page. It's low-volume but precisely aimed at EQUS's core membership — worth a direct rebuttal ad, not just brand content.
5
Borrow safety content from the wires utilities.
Fortis and ATCO prove that line-safety and storm-prep content earns cheap goodwill even with no sales CTA. Low-cost, high-trust format EQUS isn't using yet.
