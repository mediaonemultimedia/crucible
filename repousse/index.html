Relief 01

Every letter is its own ExtrudeGeometry, built once from the wordmark's real vector outlines with a full chamfered bevel baked in. At rest each letter's own Z-scale is held at zero — front and back caps collapse onto the same plane and the reverse is back-face culled — so the type reads as flat, printed 2D. Nothing here is a shader illusion: depth grows by animating that same scale toward 1, real vertex positions moving in real 3D.

Dwell 02

A per-letter accumulator charges while the cursor lingers nearby and bleeds off once it leaves — the same gain/decay dwell field Intaglio (No. 16) carves relief with, applied here to a discrete letter instead of a continuous height map.

Camera & light 03

Continuous and independent of dwell: the whole word tilts a few degrees toward the cursor. Straight-on, a fully extruded side wall is nearly edge-on and reads as almost nothing — the tilt is what actually lets its raking-light shading show.

Method A short wordmark's glyph outlines are parsed from a real vector font (converted from a TTF into the same outline mini-language Three.js's own FontLoader reads) and triangulated into one ExtrudeGeometry per letter, chamfer included. Depth lives on each letter's own object-space Z-scale, not a shader: zero is genuinely flat, geometry collapsed and the reverse culled away. A dwell accumulator drives that scale from 0 toward 1 per letter, and because the scale is real, Three's normal matrix keeps every bevel and side face correctly lit as it grows — caught flat by the key light on the front face, raking across the newly exposed sides. A continuous pointer parallax tilts the whole plate, independent of dwell, so those side faces visibly shift with viewing angle.
