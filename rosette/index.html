Halftone screen 01

Each plate is its own angled grid — C 15° · M 75° · Y 0° · K 45°, the classic offset that keeps four independent dot screens from beating against each other. Every dot's radius comes from the real scene's ink density sampled fresh at that plate's own cell — nothing here is a texture or a filter.

Flake & clearcoat 02

Every micro-facet is a random unit normal read from a 3D hash field, blended into the surface normal. The sharp specular lobe only lights up the facets currently aligned with the light/view half-vector — so which flecks sparkle changes continuously as either angle moves.

Flip-flop paint 03

The base tint isn't the flake sparkle — it's a separate Fresnel mix between a face color and a flop color, driven by the clean geometric normal. Real pearlescent automotive paint shifts hue this way as the panel tilts under a light; tilt the camera or move the light here to see the same effect.

Light & camera 04

Move the cursor over the canvas to swing the key light — continuous, no click required. Drag instead orbits the camera, which changes the Fresnel angle the same way. Space pauses the idle drift, R resets light and camera to their starting angle.
