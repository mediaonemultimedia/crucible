Attention 01

There is no rig. The head's aim is two exponential filters chasing the same target at different rates — fast for the head, slow for the body — so it snaps to notice you, then settles into facing you.

Motion 02

Nothing here is baked. Every joint position is recomputed on the CPU each frame — the distance field is the skeleton — and handed to the shader as a fresh set of primitive transforms.

Render 03

Shadows are a second march toward the key light, tracking the tightest pinch of distance ÷ travel along the way. Ambient occlusion samples the field a few short steps off the surface normal and compares it to what an empty room would give back.

Method There is no mesh and no bones. Every part of the body — spine, skull, ears, tail — is a sphere or a capsule, and the only thing that makes them read as one animal is a polynomial smooth-minimum welding their distances together where they meet. Animation is nothing but those primitives' positions changing over time: a breathing scale on the torso, two damped rotations chasing the cursor for the head, a swung pair of segments for the tail. The shader never sees a rig — it just sphere-traces whatever shape the primitives make this frame.
