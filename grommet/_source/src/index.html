Inside the experiment 06

Each athlete is a position-based soft body (small steps on a fixed 1/120 s clock): one shape-matched cloud for torso and head, with the legs sewn into it, and chains for the arms, ears and tail. Two legs and a tall plush would tip straight over, so a balance pull stands the cloud's sewn shape upright about its own centre — a roly-poly righting, no momentum of its own.

Nothing is keyframed. A stroke or a dive re-poses the sewn shape — the arm turned about the shoulder, the shoulders turned with it, arms thrown up — and shape memory hauls the stuffing after it, lagging and wobbling. The shuffle is a velocity servo on the body; getting up after a dive is a damped heave toward upright.

The racquet is a rigid body held in the paw, its handle along the forearm the physics gives. Every substep the ball is tested against the string plane and its own velocity at the contact: strings are lively, the wooden frame is dead and skews the shot. A clean contact is steered back toward you. A ball to the head is an impulse into the head's stuffing: it squashes, the headband dents with it, and he's dazed.

The fur is the body drawn 64 times as shells cut into strands, groomable with the comb, lit along each strand (Kajiya-Kay); the rosettes are printed under the pile. The kit is a second skin of cotton skinned to the same body — no fur under it — that swings on the fur-lag springs and creases where it bunches.

The goal's net is a verlet mesh pinned to its frame: the ball shoves the knots, the knots slow the ball. A save is the ball and the bear pushing each other apart by mass.
