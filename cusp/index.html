Cusp

Field 01

The cursor is raycast onto the pool plane every frame, then each lattice cell samples a Gaussian falloff from that point — a stand-in for a magnetic potential, not a real field solve. Response scales how fast a spike chases that target — it rises about 3× faster than it falls, so growth reads sharp and relaxation reads like real surface tension settling.

Lattice 02

Every spike sits on a fixed hexagonal lattice — the same near-hex packing real Rosensweig spikes settle into. Each cell is an exact round-cone SDF, nearest-cell assigned, smooth-min welded into the pool — the same blend Armature uses for its skeleton.

Material 03

No environment map is loaded — the studio panels reflected in the surface are a few procedural bright bands read off the reflection vector. Polish sharpens the highlight and lifts base reflectivity off near-black; Tone shifts the metal between a cool chrome and a warmer gunmetal.

Method There is no fluid solve here — no particles, no pressure grid. The pool is a flat signed-distance plane. A hexagonal lattice of cells each samples the field at its own position and keeps one persistent, damped height, eased toward its target every frame in plain JavaScript. Those heights are written into a tiny floating-point texture and read back with texelFetch inside the raymarcher, which builds an exact round-cone at the nearest lattice point and smooth-min welds it into the pool. The spikes are real geometry — traced, lit and reflective — just never simulated as a liquid.
